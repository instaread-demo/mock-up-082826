Accessory Focus: Brooches and Pins

— by Angie
on August 26, 2026
Listen to audio version of this article

Brooches and pins are expressive accessories. They can be subtle or statement-making, vintage or modern, whimsical or elegant, sentimental or purely decorative. Some people collect them, while others own just one or two treasured pieces. Whether you wear them frequently or only on occasion, they have a remarkable ability to add a story, sparkle, and a touch of fun to an outfit.

Although the terms are often used interchangeably, there is a subtle distinction. A brooch refers to a larger decorative piece of jewellery that fastens to clothing with a pin on the back, while pin is a broader term that also includes lapel pins, enamel pins, novelty pins, commemorative pins, and other smaller decorative pieces. All brooches are pins, but not all pins are brooches. Both can elevate an outfit in unique and personal ways.

Unlike many accessories, brooches and pins aren’t confined to one part of an outfit. That’s part of their appeal. They can be worn almost anywhere, making them one of the most versatile and creative ways to personalize your style.

The variety is enormous. Brooches and pins come in every imaginable size, shape, material, colour, finish, and price point. They can be crafted from precious metals and gemstones, enamel, resin, wood, pearls, crystals, fabric, leather, or acrylic. Designs range from flowers, animals, insects, bows, hearts, and abstract shapes to initials, humorous motifs, sculptural forms, contemporary works of wearable art, and almost anything else. Some are delicate and understated, while others are large enough to become the focal point of an outfit.

Traditionally, brooches were worn on a jacket or coat lapel, but today there are more possibilities. Pin one onto the shoulder of a sweater, the neckline or collar of a shirt, the pocket of a denim jacket, the cuff of a sleeve, a blazer, vest, scarf, hat, handbag, or even the ribbon of a straw hat. They can also look striking on the waistbands of skirts and dresses, or clustered on the front of a canvas tote. Brooches and pins can be used to hitch up skirts, tops, and pant hems. You can ruche a tee at the side, secure the ruching with a brooch, and enjoy the diagonal line and asymmetrical integrity it creates. Brooches can also be worn as pendants on necklaces. Wear one on its own for a subtle accent, or group several together for greater impact. Brooches invite creativity, and sometimes the most unexpected placement is the most successful.

Brooches can also solve practical styling challenges. They can hold a scarf in place, secure the neckline of a wrap or cardigan, gather fabric to create a different silhouette, or discreetly disguise a small stain, hole, or repair. Decorative and functional is a winning combination.

Because they’re relatively small accessories and easy to store, brooches and pins are an easy way to experiment with trends, colour, shine, or a playful motif without making a big commitment. A single statement brooch can change the mood of a simple navy blazer, denim jacket, winter coat, jumper, or hat. It can be a quick, effective, and often affordable way to refresh wardrobe favourites you may have worn for years.

Vintage and antique brooches often tell a story. They may have been inherited from a family member, discovered while travelling, found in an antique shop, or picked up at a flea market. Even contemporary brooches can become meaningful over time, reminding us of a special event, place, person, or milestone. Like many accessories, they can carry memories as well as style.

Some people enjoy matching their brooches to the colours or theme of an outfit, while others deliberately create contrast. A colourful enamel pin on a monochromatic outfit, a sparkling crystal brooch on casual denim, or a whimsical motif on classic tailoring can add unexpected interest and create pleasing visual tension. Sometimes that element of surprise is exactly what makes an outfit memorable.

If you’ve never worn brooches or pins before, they can feel unfamiliar at first. Start with one that genuinely appeals to you and wear it on a jacket or coat. As you become more comfortable, experiment with different placements and combinations. Wear a cluster of brooches, if you like. Or stick to one simple way of wearing a fabulous brooch. There is no right or wrong approach. It’s about finding a way of wearing brooches and pins that feels authentic to your style and makes you happy.

I have exactly ONE brooch. It’s the best-brooch-for-me on the planet. It was a gift, and you can read the story here. I would like to wear it more frequently, and plan to do just that. That said, I derive as much joy from looking at my special brooch displayed in a jewellery box. It’s a vintage piece from 1960, a beautiful work of art, and makes me smile.

Over to you. Have you found your brooch wearing balance?
