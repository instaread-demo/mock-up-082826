Press Release for the Unsanctioned Invasion of My Wife’s Side of the Bed

This is not the first incident along the border to spark conflict.

At 1100 hours last night, there was a flare-up and escalation of the long running border conflict between my side of the bed and my wife’s side of the bed. It began with good intentions, as a peacekeeping mission to protect the inhabitant on my side from the wanton aggression of East Mattress, but the brief-planned occupation may develop into a more permanent stay depending on comfort levels.

Negotiations were opened months ago into the development of more usable land for both sides of the border by the jettisoning of decorative pillows and throw blankets at the bed’s foot, to no avail. Yes, this is not the first incident along the border to spark conflict. West Mattress remembers a recent war of words that degenerated into small arms fire, and despite our best efforts to get back to the gym, our small arms have not improved. East Mattress has threatened to blockade a vital economic pipeline in response, but frankly we weren’t making much money on Facebook Marketplace anyway.

We do not take such actions lightly, only in the interest of international security. Our national intelligence network recently intercepted a text from her friend that came up on screen while we were streaming something. This data suggests she is enriching material—Amazon rewards points—for weapons of mass destruction: a body pillow that would take up a third of the bed.

My wife’s burning feet keep crossing to my side and making me uncomfortable. What had largely been a Cold War of positioning and empty threats has now gone hot. The root of the conflict, like so many modern combats, is an outgrowth of environmental change—the A/C is set too low—and competition for limited resources in the form of elbow room.

Border security has long been a concern in West Mattress for humanitarian reasons. While we have always sent aid across the divide in the form of passing tissues, an open border could allow the disease outbreak there to develop into a house-wide pandemic. My wife’s repeated incursions in the past have led to ceasefires—as when the baby started crying and we both agreed to get up and deal with it—but few permanent solutions.

Efforts by international organizations to broker a more lasting settlement have failed, such as when her parents offered to buy a bigger mattress and the autocrat of West Mattress demurred, too prideful to accept aid. Well-intentioned proposals of a two-state solution resulted in a tearful misunderstanding that required West Mattress to send a flower delivery as reparations.

East Mattress has long coveted the more optimal nightstand situation in West Mattress, ever since I took that business trip and she had the whole bed to herself. Despite East Mattress’ attempt to deter us with chemical warfare—a disagreeable new Glade scent plugin in her outlet—our strike force of ten toes made it into hostile territory without much opposition. Now begins the slow encroachment of our military’s main body—and our literal main body—into defensible positions which we can hold once she wakes up and finds herself forced to the brink of the bedframe.

As of press time, peace negotiations have stalled over an inability to decide on who is getting up first to adjust the A/C. The United Nations of our family group chat has called the whole affair a sordid war crime. I wouldn’t know. I muted that text thread years ago.
